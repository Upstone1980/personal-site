From Experiment to Expectation

Private 5G in Australian Industry

28 September 2026

Australia's major industrial sectors have always operated at the edge — remote locations, harsh conditions, zero tolerance for communications failure. What has changed is the technology agenda sitting on top of that.

Autonomous equipment, real-time operational data, streaming video, integrated IT and OT environments — these cannot be supported at scale on Wi-Fi or public mobile networks alone. Private 5G networks give operators the performance, security, and control they need to run these environments on their own terms.

A Market Past the Tipping Point

We are now in a market that has moved past proof of concept. The early adopters — tier one miners, rail operators, and energy companies — demonstrated the value, and the rest of the industry has taken notice. Decisions that were once exploratory are now strategic. Customers come to us with a clearer brief than they did two or three years ago. They know what they need, they understand the technology options, and they want a partner who can deliver end to end.

"When connectivity drops, tonnes stop moving, and people are at risk."

Dual Capability Matters

Most private network providers come from either the enterprise IT world or the telco world. BAI Communications sits in a different position. We have been designing, deploying and operating mission-critical communications infrastructure for government and emergency services for many years. The NSW Public Safety Network connects more than 50 government agencies and 75,000 emergency service terminals across 540,000 square kilometres.

That experience shapes how we approach industrial private networks. When you have maintained 99.999% availability through the Black Summer bushfires and Cyclone Alfred, you develop a specific understanding of what resilience means.

From Mine to Port

Titan ICT's experience spanned the mining value chain, from production at the pit through processing, rail, and port. Now, as part of BAI Communications, we can offer clients an end-to-end service — from spectrum licensing and network design through to deployment and ongoing managed support — backed by people with deep operational experience in these environments.

BAI and the former Titan team are already helping miners modernise connectivity with private 5G and advanced wireless across sites and ports, with the connecting rail corridors the next focus. The goal is seamless data, voice and video communications from mine to port, supporting greater automation, visibility, and safe, efficient movement of ore.

Network as a Service

Unusual in the industry, BAI can own the network infrastructure and operate it on behalf of a client, allowing mining and energy operators to focus on their core business activities and 'subscribe' to connectivity rather than building and maintaining their own highly specialised networks. For the resource sector, where capital is increasingly directed toward extraction rather than infrastructure, this model is gaining serious traction.

This post draws on conversations and themes from my Q&A with RCR Wireless News (July 2026) and Resources Review (June 2026).
